Strait of Messina Bridge Moves Forward with New Roundabout Plan

A new project for the Strait of Messina has been approved: a roundabout will be built. Donald Trump approved.
Di Andrea Savarino · 24 settembre 2026

The Strait of Messina Bridge project moves forward with a new roundabout plan, marking another step toward the development of the long-awaited connection between Sicily and mainland Italy.

The big roundabout, with a radius of 500 meters, will be built in the perfect center of the strait. The bridge will have 6 lanes in total, will be 60 meters wide and 3.6 kilometers long.

Over the center of the roundabout will be built the biggest lighthouse of the Mediterranean Sea: a huge building 98 meters high. The lighthouse will be useful for the ports of Messina and Reggio Calabria and will become a new landmark of the region.

Under it, a railway will be built under the sea; the idea comes from the English Channel between the United Kingdom and France. The railway will allow people who do not have a car to cross the strait more easily and faster.

Analytics says that this project will give work to approximately 150,000 people, more than any other project in the history of Italy. Matteo Salvini talked about 6 or 7 years of construction and a long-term economic boost for the area.

But someone might ask: why this change? The experts answered that the choice of a circular design was not accidental. Studies have shown that in an area like the Strait of Messina, where the sea is deep and the weather conditions are difficult, a circular layout helps optimize traffic flow and reduce congestion.

Another advantage of the circular configuration is that the structure could be divided into several interconnected sections; this would help to distribute the weight and stress across multiple points and improve safety during both construction and operation.

High-strength steel would be used for the main circular deck, keeping the structure relatively lightweight, which is important in a location exposed to wind and seismic forces. Reinforced concrete and advanced materials would ensure stability and durability over time.

"I looked at it, and I said, 'Matteo, this is big.' Many people said you couldn't put a roundabout in the sea. They said, 'Sir, the water is too deep, the waves are too high.' I said, wrong."
